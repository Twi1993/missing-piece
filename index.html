How I did it

I had no healthcare experience. My career has been in sales and social services, and everything I know about healthcare I learned through WGU. Sales turned out to be my advantage. My degree was a business degree, and a lot of what WGU tested me on, I had already learned on the job: negotiating, managing accounts, reading people, hitting numbers.

In April 2025 I started on Sophia Learning to knock out transfer credits. Two weeks in, my car broke down. It took two weeks to get it fixed, and it killed my motivation. I stopped. That was the real test, not any exam.

I got past that mental block by keeping one thought in front of me: once this degree is done, it's done. Nobody can take it back, and I never have to do it again. So I went back to work.

Four months of hard work, then finished. At least until my master's.

Along the way, my WGU courses got me hooked on supply chain. Today I work as a logistics professional, and I wrote this guide so you can skip the months of digging I did and spend that energy on the work itself.

Keep your mental game up. Something will knock you off track: a car, a kid, a bad week at work. Plan for it. The people who finish aren't the ones who never stop. They're the ones who start again.

Questions

Is 4 months realistic for most people?

Not for everyone. It worked for me with transfer credits, a clear plan, a sales background that matched the material, and steady effort. Many people take two terms. The goal is as few terms as you can manage.

How many Sophia courses will I need?

I took 19. WGU's transfer list changes, so check WGU's partner page and your enrollment counselor for the current list for your program.

Is WGU accredited?

Yes, by the Northwest Commission on Colleges and Universities (NWCCU).

Are you affiliated with WGU or Sophia?

No. Missing Degree is independent and isn't endorsed by either. Their names are used only to describe them.

Do I need to quit my job?

No. Both are online and self-paced. I worked full-time the whole way.

What if I don't qualify for Pell?

Plan on about $4,250 for a one-term path. The guide covers scholarships and other options.

What if I lose motivation?

You probably will at some point. I did. The 90-day emails are built for that week, and the guide has a section on getting restarted.
